The rapid proliferation of IoT and embedded systems in Latin America has created a critical vulnerability: the need for lightweight, high-performance cryptographic standards that ensure data integrity without compromising hardware efficiency. My research addresses this gap through the development of FCH-ARX V4, a cryptographic hash algorithm designed for optimized security. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, the algorithm has successfully passed NIST FIPS 180-4 validation, demonstrating that original, high-impact security research can be developed from within the region.

My unique academic profile—a double degree in Economics (UTM) and Data Science & AI Engineering (U. Guayaquil)—allows me to approach this problem from a rare multidisciplinary perspective. While my economic training provides a rigorous foundation in causal inference and econometric modeling, my engineering background enables the technical implementation of LLM-driven agentic architectures and MLOps. This synergy is essential for developing AI-driven security protocols that are not only mathematically sound but computationally viable.

However, a significant gap exists between my current professional reality and my research potential. While serving as a Commercial Advisor at Vamoret S.A., I have developed FCH-ARX V4 independently. Transitioning from a corporate role to a full-time academic environment via the OAS Scholarship is the catalyst I need to move from independent experimentation to formal, publishable scientific contribution.

With the support of the OAS, my research plan is twofold: first, to integrate the FCH-ARX V4 algorithm into AI-powered autonomous systems to secure multi-agent communications; and second, to develop a framework for "Intelligent Cryptography" where ML optimizes hash functions for resource-constrained embedded devices. This funding will allow me to dedicate 100% of my capacity to advancing Latin America’s sovereignty in cybersecurity and AI, transforming theoretical mathematical models into scalable, real-world infrastructure.
