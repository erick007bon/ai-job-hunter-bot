The security of IoT and embedded systems is currently bottlenecked by the trade-off between cryptographic robustness and computational efficiency. My research focuses on solving this through the development of lightweight, high-performance hashing algorithms capable of securing resource-constrained devices without compromising integrity. This pursuit led me to develop FCH-ARX V4, a cryptographic hash algorithm that successfully passed NIST FIPS 180-4 validation, demonstrating a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance.

Despite this breakthrough, my current role as a Commercial Advisor at Vamoret S.A. creates a significant professional gap. While I have pushed the boundaries of cryptography independently, the transition from an isolated developer to a rigorous AI researcher requires the structured academic environment and specialized mentorship that the OAS Academic Scholarships Program provides. This funding is the essential catalyst that would allow me to shift from part-time experimentation to full-time research.

My unique strength lies in my dual academic formation. By combining an Economics degree from UTM with my ongoing studies in Data Science and AI Engineering at U. Guayaquil, I possess a rare hybrid perspective: the econometric rigor to analyze systemic risk and the technical capability to implement MLOps, PyTorch, and multi-agent systems via Model Context Protocol (MCP). This interdisciplinarity allows me to approach AI not just as a coding task, but as a mathematical optimization problem.

With this scholarship, my research plan is to evolve FCH-ARX V4 into a framework for AI-driven autonomous security protocols for decentralized systems. I aim to integrate machine learning to dynamically optimize cryptographic parameters based on real-time network threats. By securing the data layer of Latin American digital infrastructure, I intend to contribute to the region's technological sovereignty and economic stability, transforming my original research into scalable, publishable contributions to the global scientific community.
