The current landscape of IoT and embedded systems faces a critical vulnerability: the lack of lightweight yet robust cryptographic primitives capable of securing edge devices without compromising computational efficiency. My research addresses this gap through the development of FCH-ARX V4, a cryptographic hash algorithm designed for high-performance security. Having achieved a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance—validating against NIST FIPS 180-4 standards—I have demonstrated that original, rigorous cryptographic research is possible from Latin America.

However, my trajectory has been one of resilience and self-reliance. While my current role as a Commercial Advisor at Vamoret S.A. provides professional stability, it creates a systemic gap between my intellectual capacity and my daily activities. I have developed FCH-ARX V4 and my expertise in LLM multi-agent protocols independently, balancing full-time employment with rigorous self-study. The OAS Scholarship is the catalyst I need to transition from an independent developer to a full-time researcher, allowing me to dedicate my entire cognitive bandwidth to advancing AI-driven security.

My unique strength lies in my dual academic foundation. Combining an Economics degree from UTM with my ongoing Data Science and AI Engineering studies at the University of Guayaquil allows me to approach AI not just as a technical challenge, but as an optimization problem. This interdisciplinary lens—blending econometric causal inference with MLOps and deep learning—enables me to design systems that are both mathematically sound and economically viable.

With this funding, I intend to pursue a Master’s degree focusing on the integration of lightweight cryptography with AI-driven anomaly detection for IoT networks. My goal is to publish my current findings in peer-reviewed journals and develop a framework for autonomous, secure edge computing. By bridging the gap between high-level AI and low-level security, I aim to contribute to the technological sovereignty and digital resilience of the OAS member states.
