The scalability of the Internet of Things (IoT) in developing economies is currently hindered by a critical trade-off: the need for robust security versus the limited computational power of embedded systems. Standard cryptographic protocols are often too resource-intensive for low-power sensors. My research addresses this gap by developing lightweight, high-efficiency hashing algorithms that ensure data integrity without sacrificing performance.

My development of FCH-ARX V4 serves as a proof of concept for this ambition. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance—passing NIST FIPS 180-4 validation—I have demonstrated my capacity to conduct original, publishable research in cryptography. However, my current role as a Commercial Advisor at Vamoret S.A. creates a systemic gap; while I possess the technical proficiency, my research is pursued independently in my spare time. The DAAD scholarship is the essential catalyst I need to transition from self-taught experimentation to formal, full-time academic rigor in Germany’s world-leading STEM ecosystem.

My unique strength lies in my multidisciplinary profile. Combining a degree in Economics with ongoing studies in Data Science and AI Engineering allows me to approach AI not just as a technical tool, but as a socio-economic lever. I can analyze the cost-benefit efficiency of AI deployments while architecting the underlying ML pipelines and MLOps infrastructure.

With this funding, I intend to specialize in the intersection of AI and cybersecurity. My objective is to integrate LLM-driven automation and multi-agent systems (via MCP protocols) to optimize the deployment of lightweight cryptographic frameworks in industrial IoT networks. By leveraging Germany's expertise in engineering and AI, I aim to develop secure, automated data architectures that can be exported back to Ecuador to modernize national digital infrastructure, ensuring that Latin American technological growth is both scalable and secure.
