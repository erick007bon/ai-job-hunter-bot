The critical vulnerability of IoT and embedded systems lies in the computational trade-off between robust security and energy efficiency. Standard cryptographic protocols often exceed the resource constraints of edge devices, leaving a gap that exposes critical infrastructure to systemic risks. My research focuses on optimizing this balance through the development of lightweight, high-performance cryptographic primitives enhanced by machine learning.

My primary contribution, the FCH-ARX V4 algorithm, addresses this challenge. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance—passing NIST FIPS 180-4 validation—I have demonstrated that original, mathematically rigorous security architectures are possible outside traditional academic hubs. However, developing these systems independently while working as a Commercial Advisor has created a ceiling for my productivity. The Google Research Scholar Program would bridge this gap, allowing me to transition from fragmented, self-taught exploration to a full-time, rigorous research trajectory.

My dual background in Economics and Data Science & AI Engineering provides a unique analytical lens: I combine the formal logic of econometric causal inference with the technical agility of MLOps and PyTorch. This interdisciplinary perspective allows me to approach security not just as a coding problem, but as an optimization problem of resource allocation and systemic stability.

With this funding, I intend to evolve FCH-ARX V4 into a framework for "Adaptive Cryptography." My plan is to implement an AI-driven layer that dynamically adjusts encryption complexity based on real-time threat levels and device battery health, reducing energy overhead without compromising integrity. I will focus on utilizing Model Context Protocol (MCP) to create multi-agent systems capable of automating the auditing and stress-testing of these algorithms. This research aligns directly with Google’s mission of advancing Security, Privacy, and Abuse Prevention, ensuring that the next generation of connected devices remains secure, scalable, and efficient.
