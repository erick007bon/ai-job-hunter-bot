The proliferation of IoT and embedded systems has created a critical vulnerability: the need for lightweight, collision-resistant cryptographic primitives that do not compromise device performance. My current research focuses on optimizing the intersection of hashing efficiency and security, specifically addressing the trade-off between computational overhead and cryptographic strength in resource-constrained environments.

My development of the FCH-ARX V4 algorithm serves as a proof of concept for this objective. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and 2^256 collision resistance, validated against NIST FIPS 180-4 standards, I have demonstrated that original, high-performance cryptographic structures are possible. This work aligns directly with Google’s mission in "Security, Privacy, and Abuse Prevention," as the next evolution of this research involves integrating Machine Learning to detect and prevent collision attacks in real-time.

Currently, I navigate a significant gap: I conduct this research independently while working full-time as a Commercial Advisor. While this professional experience has honed my discipline, the lack of dedicated time and infrastructure limits the scaling of my findings. Receiving this scholarship would transition me from a self-taught researcher to a full-time innovator, allowing me to bridge the gap between theoretical cryptography and scalable AI implementation.

My double academic background in Economics and Data Science & AI Engineering provides a unique lens; I approach system optimization not just as a coding challenge, but as an econometric problem of resource allocation and efficiency. 

With this funding, I plan to develop a framework that utilizes LLM-driven agents and MCP protocols to automate the fuzzing and vulnerability testing of my FCH-ARX iterations, aiming to publish a peer-reviewed paper on "AI-Optimized Lightweight Cryptography for Edge Computing." I am committed to contributing to the global security ecosystem by creating tools that ensure data integrity without sacrificing the agility of the next generation of smart devices.
