The critical vulnerability of IoT and embedded systems lies in the trade-off between cryptographic robustness and computational overhead. Current standards often struggle to maintain high security without compromising performance in resource-constrained environments. My research addresses this gap by optimizing the intersection of cryptographic hash functions and machine learning to create adaptive, lightweight security protocols that prevent collisions and resist quantum-era attacks.

My development of the FCH-ARX V4 algorithm serves as a proof of concept for this approach. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, the algorithm has successfully passed NIST FIPS 180-4 validation. This work aligns directly with Google Research’s mission in 'Security, Privacy, and Abuse Prevention,' specifically in developing scalable, verifiable systems that protect data integrity across decentralized networks.

My trajectory is unconventional but strategic. With a double academic background in Economics (UTM) and Data Science & AI (U. Guayaquil), I combine econometric rigor—specifically causal inference and time-series analysis—with deep learning architectures. This duality allows me to analyze security not just as a technical challenge, but as an optimization problem of resource allocation and risk. However, pursuing this research independently while working as a Commercial Advisor has created a structural bottleneck. This scholarship would bridge that gap, allowing me to transition from a professional role into a dedicated research trajectory.

With the $60,000 funding, I plan to evolve FCH-ARX V4 into a hybrid framework: integrating LLM-driven agents for automated vulnerability detection and utilizing the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to create multi-agent systems that autonomously optimize cryptographic parameters in real-time based on network threat levels. My goal is to publish these findings in top-tier venues (NeurIPS or USENIX) and contribute to the open-source security ecosystem, ensuring that high-level encryption is accessible for the next generation of edge computing.
