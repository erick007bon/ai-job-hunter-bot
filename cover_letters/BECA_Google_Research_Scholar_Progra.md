The current proliferation of IoT and embedded systems has created a critical vulnerability: the struggle to implement robust cryptographic security within severe hardware constraints. Most standard algorithms are too computationally expensive for low-power devices, leaving the edge layer exposed to systemic abuse. My research addresses this gap by optimizing the balance between collision resistance and computational efficiency.

My development of the FCH-ARX V4 cryptographic hash algorithm represents a significant step forward in this domain. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and ensuring $2^{256}$ collision resistance—validating against NIST FIPS 180-4 standards—I have demonstrated that high-security primitives can be engineered for maximum performance. This work aligns directly with Google Research’s mission of "Security, Privacy, and Abuse Prevention," specifically in developing scalable, secure systems that protect user data at the hardware level.

My trajectory is unconventional. I hold a degree in Economics from UTM and am currently pursuing Data Science and AI Engineering at U. Guayaquil. This dual background allows me to approach security not just as a technical problem, but as a resource-optimization challenge. However, while I currently serve as a Commercial Advisor at Vamoret S.A., this role is decoupled from my research. I have pursued my breakthroughs independently, driven by rigor and curiosity, but I have reached a ceiling. This scholarship would bridge the gap, allowing me to transition from a part-time independent researcher to a full-time innovator.

With this funding, I plan to evolve FCH-ARX V4 by integrating Machine Learning to analyze and harden the algorithm against differential cryptanalysis. I intend to develop a framework for "Adaptive Cryptography," where AI dynamically adjusts encryption overhead based on the device's real-time power constraints. This integration of AI and cryptography will ensure that the next generation of embedded systems is secure by design, not by chance.
