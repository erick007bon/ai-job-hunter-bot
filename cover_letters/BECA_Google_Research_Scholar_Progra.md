The critical bottleneck in securing the burgeoning IoT and embedded systems ecosystem is the trade-off between cryptographic strength and computational overhead. Current standards often exceed the resource capacities of low-power devices, creating vulnerabilities in edge computing. My research addresses this by developing lightweight, high-performance cryptographic primitives that maintain rigorous security guarantees without compromising latency.

My development of the FCH-ARX V4 algorithm serves as a proof of concept for this approach. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, the algorithm successfully passed NIST FIPS 180-4 validation. This work aligns directly with the Google Research Scholar Program’s focus on "Security, Privacy and Abuse Prevention," specifically in the pursuit of robust, scalable security architectures that can withstand adversarial attacks in decentralized environments.

My trajectory is unconventional: I hold a degree in Economics and am currently completing a degree in Data Science and AI Engineering. This duality allows me to approach AI not just as a technical challenge, but as a system of optimization and risk management. However, my current role as a Commercial Advisor at Vamoret S.A. creates a significant gap between my professional obligations and my research potential. Pursuing this research independently while working full-time has limited my ability to scale these findings into peer-reviewed publications and production-ready frameworks.

With the $60,000 grant, I intend to transition into full-time research to evolve FCH-ARX V4 into an AI-driven adaptive security layer. My concrete plan is to implement a machine learning framework that dynamically adjusts cryptographic intensity based on real-time threat telemetry, utilizing LLMs and multi-agent systems (via MCP) to automate vulnerability detection in embedded firmware. This funding will transform a promising original algorithm into a scalable, published contribution to the global security community, bridging the gap between theoretical cryptography and applied AI.
