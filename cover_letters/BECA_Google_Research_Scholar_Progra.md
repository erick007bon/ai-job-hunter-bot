The fundamental challenge in securing the burgeoning IoT and embedded systems ecosystem lies in the inefficiency of current cryptographic standards, which often demand computational resources exceeding the capacity of low-power hardware. My research focuses on bridging this gap by developing lightweight, high-performance cryptographic primitives that maintain rigorous security guarantees without compromising system latency.

My development of the FCH-ARX V4 algorithm serves as a proof of concept for this ambition. By achieving NIST FIPS 180-4 validation with a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, I have demonstrated the ability to engineer original, mathematically sound security protocols. This work aligns directly with Google Research’s mission in "Security, Privacy and Abuse Prevention," particularly in creating resilient systems that protect user data at the edge.

Currently, I navigate a significant structural gap: while I possess the technical capacity to push this research forward, I am working full-time as a Commercial Advisor to sustain my studies. This professional commitment limits my capacity for the deep, iterative experimentation that high-level ML and security research requires. This scholarship would provide the critical financial runway to transition from independent experimentation to full-time research.

My dual background in Economics and Data Science provides a unique competitive edge; I combine the rigorous causal inference and optimization frameworks of econometrics with the scalable implementation power of AI and MLOps. This interdisciplinarity allows me to analyze security not just as a technical problem, but as an optimization problem of resource allocation and risk.

With this funding, I intend to integrate my FCH-ARX V4 framework with an ML-driven optimization layer to automatically tune cryptographic parameters based on real-time hardware constraints. My goal is to publish a peer-reviewed framework for "Adaptive Lightweight Cryptography," ensuring that security is not a bottleneck for the next generation of intelligent, decentralized systems.
