The current bottleneck in securing IoT and embedded systems is the tension between high-level cryptographic security and limited computational resources. My research focuses on bridging this gap by developing lightweight, high-performance hashing algorithms that maintain rigorous security standards without compromising latency. This led me to create FCH-ARX V4, a cryptographic hash algorithm that achieved a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, successfully passing NIST FIPS 180-4 validation.

My profile exists at the intersection of Economics and AI Engineering—a dual academic background that allows me to approach machine learning not just as a coding challenge, but as a problem of optimization and resource allocation. While I currently serve as a Commercial Advisor at Vamoret S.A., I have spent my nights and weekends independently building end-to-end AI architectures, from PyTorch predictive models to multi-agent systems utilizing the Model Context Protocol (MCP). However, the ceiling for independent research is limited by infrastructure and peer collaboration. This residency represents the vital bridge I need to transition from a self-taught researcher to a full-time AI contributor.

At OpenAI, I intend to investigate the synergy between cryptographic primitives and LLM security. Specifically, I plan to research how lightweight hashing and zero-knowledge proofs can be integrated into the weights or communication layers of distributed agentic systems to ensure data integrity and privacy in multi-agent environments. By combining my expertise in econometrics (causal inference) and cryptography with OpenAI’s compute power, I aim to develop frameworks that make AI agents not only more capable but provably secure. I am eager to bring this unique Latin American perspective to San Francisco, contributing a rigorous, interdisciplinary approach to the frontier of AGI.
