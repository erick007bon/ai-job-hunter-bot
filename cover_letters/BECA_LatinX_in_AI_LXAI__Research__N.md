The security of IoT and embedded systems is currently hindered by a critical trade-off: high-security cryptographic standards are often too computationally expensive for resource-constrained hardware. This creates a vulnerability gap that my current research seeks to bridge. I have developed **FCH-ARX V4**, an original cryptographic hash algorithm designed for efficiency without compromising security. Through rigorous testing, it has passed NIST FIPS 180-4 validation, achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, proving its viability for secure, low-power implementations.

My profile is an unconventional hybrid; holding a degree in Economics (UTM) while pursuing Data Science and AI Engineering (U. Guayaquil) allows me to approach AI research with a unique lens of optimization and quantitative rigor. While I possess the technical stack to build end-to-end AI solutions—from PyTorch models to multi-agent systems via Model Context Protocol (MCP)—my current role as a Commercial Advisor creates a significant gap between my professional daily routine and my research ambitions. I am currently researching independently, but the LatinX in AI scholarship would provide the mentorship and networking infrastructure necessary to transition from a self-taught researcher to a contributor in the global AI community.

Being part of LXAI would allow me to refine FCH-ARX V4 under the guidance of top-tier experts and present my findings at conferences like NeurIPS or ICML. My concrete goal is to integrate this cryptographic framework into federated learning architectures to ensure data privacy in decentralized AI systems. With this scholarship, I plan to formalize my research into a peer-reviewed publication, leverage LXAI’s mentorship to optimize my algorithm's complexity, and connect with researchers at the frontier of AI security. I am eager to represent the Ecuadorian research community and contribute to the visibility of Latin American talent in the global AI ecosystem.
