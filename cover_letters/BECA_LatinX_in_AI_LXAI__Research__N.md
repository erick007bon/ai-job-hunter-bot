The scalability of Artificial Intelligence in IoT and embedded systems is currently bottlenecked by the tension between computational efficiency and cryptographic security. As we move toward edge computing, the need for lightweight yet collision-resistant hashing algorithms is critical. My research addresses this gap through the development of **FCH-ARX V4**, a cryptographic hash algorithm designed for high-performance environments. Rigorous validation against NIST FIPS 180-4 standards confirms a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, proving that robust security can be achieved without compromising systemic latency.

My academic trajectory is uniquely multidisciplinary; holding a degree in Economics from UTM and pursuing a degree in Data Science & AI Engineering at the University of Guayaquil allows me to approach AI not just as a coder, but as a researcher focused on optimization and resource allocation. This dual perspective enables me to bridge the gap between theoretical econometric modeling and scalable MLOps.

However, a significant professional gap exists: while my current role as a Commercial Advisor provides stability, it is disconnected from my research aspirations. I have developed FCH-ARX V4 independently, balancing full-time employment with rigorous self-study in PyTorch and Model Context Protocol (MCP). The LatinX in AI scholarship is the catalyst I need to transition from independent experimentation to formal research.

With the support of LXAI, my objective is to submit FCH-ARX V4 to a top-tier conference (such as NeurIPS or ICML) and engage with the global community to refine the algorithm's integration into multi-agent systems. Specifically, I plan to leverage the mentorship and networking opportunities to collaborate with leading researchers on the intersection of cryptography and AI security. This scholarship will allow me to shift my focus from commercial operations to contributing high-impact, Latin American-led research to the global AI ecosystem.
