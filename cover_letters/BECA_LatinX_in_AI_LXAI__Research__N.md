The security of IoT and embedded systems is currently throttled by a critical trade-off: the computational cost of robust cryptography versus the limited energy resources of edge devices. My research focuses on solving this bottleneck through the development of high-efficiency cryptographic primitives that maintain rigorous security standards without compromising performance.

To address this, I developed **FCH-ARX V4**, a cryptographic hash algorithm designed for optimized throughput. Rigorous validation against NIST FIPS 180-4 standards demonstrates a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, confirming its viability for secure, resource-constrained environments. While the mathematical foundation is solid, the next leap requires integrating this with AI-driven cryptanalysis and automated optimization—a transition that aligns perfectly with the LatinX in AI mission of elevating Latin American contributions to top-tier AI research.

My trajectory is non-linear. I hold a degree in Economics and am currently pursuing my second degree in Data Science & AI Engineering. This duality allows me to approach AI not just as a coder, but as a researcher capable of blending econometrics, causal inference, and machine learning. However, while I currently work as a Commercial Advisor to sustain myself, my true intellectual investment is in independent research. The gap between my current professional role and my academic ambitions is wide; this scholarship would provide the mentorship and network necessary to transition from an independent developer to a published AI researcher.

With the support of LXAI, my goal is to refine FCH-ARX V4 through adversarial machine learning testing and submit the findings to a conference like NeurIPS or ICML. By connecting with the LXAI community, I intend to leverage multi-agent systems and LLMs to automate the verification of cryptographic primitives, ensuring that Latin American innovation leads the way in securing the next generation of intelligent hardware.
