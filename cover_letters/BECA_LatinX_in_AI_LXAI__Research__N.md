The critical bottleneck in deploying secure AI for IoT and embedded systems is the trade-off between computational overhead and cryptographic robustness. Many lightweight algorithms sacrifice collision resistance for speed, leaving edge-AI devices vulnerable. My research addresses this gap through the development of **FCH-ARX V4**, a cryptographic hash algorithm designed for high-efficiency environments. By achieving a 49.95% avalanche effect and $2^{256}$ collision resistance, FCH-ARX V4 passes NIST FIPS 180-4 validation, providing a scalable foundation for secure data integrity in decentralized AI systems.

Integrating this research into the LatinX in AI community is a pivotal step toward transforming this original work into a peer-reviewed publication. My profile is uniquely positioned at the intersection of Economics and Data Science; while my econometric background provides a rigorous framework for causal inference and statistical validation, my engineering training allows me to implement end-to-end MLOps and multi-agent architectures via MCP. This dual perspective enables me to analyze the economic feasibility of AI deployment while optimizing the underlying technical security.

Currently, I face a significant structural challenge: I conduct my research independently while working as a Commercial Advisor. While my professional role demonstrates my discipline, it creates a resource gap that hinders my ability to engage with the global research vanguard. The LXAI scholarship would bridge this divide, transitioning me from isolated experimentation to active contribution within a high-level academic network.

If awarded this scholarship, my plan is twofold: first, to refine the mathematical proofs of FCH-ARX V4 under the guidance of LXAI mentors to ensure a successful submission to a top-tier conference like NeurIPS or ICML. Second, to leverage the networking opportunities to explore how cryptographic hashes can optimize secure federated learning. My goal is to represent Ecuador in the global AI discourse, proving that high-impact, original cryptographic research can emerge from Latin American independent researchers.
